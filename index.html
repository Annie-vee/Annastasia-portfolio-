Case study 2

Akwete Pay: recovering 312 inactive merchants

Akwete Pay, a Lagos-based fintech, saw monthly volume in its Onitsha Main Market cluster (2,400 merchants) fall 21%, from ₦6.2bn to ₦4.9bn, while the failed-transaction rate rose from 2% to 7%. 312 merchants had gone inactive for 30 days, about 3.25× the normal quarterly level. Head Office suspected a rival. I built a recovery and account-growth plan based on what merchants actually said.

Finding

Of 40 inactive merchants interviewed, 45% cited late settlement and 27.5% slow reversals. Only 17.5% cited a competitor offer.

Priority account

Ifeoma Superstores fell from ₦48m to ₦17m a month (−64.6%) with 3 pending reversals worth ₦410k, even though its terminal was still active.

Operating rule

Match the action to the verified cause. Don’t lead with discounts when the main issues are service-related.

ValidatePrioritiseContactDiagnoseResolveReactivate
A contact attempt is not a recovery. A first successful transaction is reactivation; sustained activity is retention.

Also covered: two parallel workstreams (fix service problems, recover merchants), a 90-day plan with daily, twice-weekly and weekly review cadences, a six-step first-response plan for Mrs Eze, KPIs (contact rate, reactivation, 30-day retention, volume recovered), a goal of reactivating 25% of the cohort (78 merchants) in 30 days, an 80% volume recovery target for Ifeoma (₦38.4m), a HubSpot recovery view, and a risk and controls table.

Case study 3

Sunrise Digital MFB: one joined-up customer experience review

Sunrise Digital MFB had pushed hard into digital account opening through its Sunrise Pocket app. As a group, we had four weeks to give the Board one connected review of onboarding, customer feedback and the full customer journey, rather than three separate fixes.

Onboarding

Only 12% of registered customers were active after 30 days. 11,350 were lost at BVN/NIN verification, and 52.99% dropped off between accepting terms and first deposit.

Voice of the customer

Feedback sat in four channels (112 calls, 89 app reviews, 260 social comments, agent notebooks) with no single owner. I proposed a capture-to-close-the-loop process with an inbox, theme dashboard and action tracker.

Customer journey

Mapped through a customer persona, Emeka Obi, from hopeful at awareness to guarded at loyalty. Key fixes: save-and-resume verification, transaction status and reference numbers, and real service recovery.

Case study

Okonkwo FinTech Solutions: BukkiBooks

Turning usage data into an early-warning system for Customer Success. Prepared by Annastasia Ogochukwu Awoke.

1. The case

Ihuoma Fashion House operates 3 physical stores and an online shop and had used BukkiBooks for 8 months. Customer Success Executive Chiamaka Nwosu reviews the usage dashboard weekly and notices that the business has stopped using the Inventory Reconciliation feature for 5 weeks.

The new store manager had not been trained on the feature, so staff went back to a manual spreadsheet. Chiamaka investigates and finds that the customer has not left BukkiBooks: an important feature is unused because a new team member does not know how to use it. She arranges training, shares a guide, follows up and introduces the bulk-upload tool.

Two months later the customer is still using BukkiBooks. At year end, the founder, Emeka Ihuoma, renews and upgrades, explaining that he stayed because someone paid attention to how his business was using the product.

2. The bigger problem

A customer can appear active while an important part of the product is no longer used. The question is not “Is the customer using BukkiBooks?” but “Is the customer using BukkiBooks in a way that shows they are getting value from it?”

It is also a scalability problem. Customer Success cannot manually check every customer’s usage every week, so Okonkwo needs usage data to show which accounts may need attention.

3. My task

  1. Research how fintech and SaaS companies serving Nigerian SMEs use product usage data to identify at-risk or under-served customers.
  2. Design a scalable Customer Success process to identify accounts like Ihuoma earlier without reviewing every account every week, considering automation, segmentation and prioritisation.
  3. Distinguish Customer Success activities from Support activities and explain how both teams should collaborate.
  4. Identify at least one risk or limitation of using product usage data and explain how Okonkwo could address it.

4. Research focus

I looked at what usage data can tell a company, which behaviours can be monitored, how changes in usage signal that a customer may need attention, how companies find adoption gaps, how this supports Customer Success, how technology helps manage customers at scale, and the limits of relying on usage data alone. I researched three companies.

Moniepoint

Monitors usage and business activity: login frequency, transaction volume, feature adoption, support ticket trends, engagement signals and changes in transaction patterns. Customer-facing roles use these to find adoption gaps and disengagement, then intervene through training, guidance and targeted support.

Paystack

Gives merchants detail on customer and transaction behaviour: customer growth, new and returning customers, payments, amount spent, and successful, failed, reversed and abandoned transactions, filterable over different periods.

Bumpa

Gives businesses analytics on customers, orders, average spend, purchasing behaviour and product performance across periods, and lets them create customer groups based on behaviour and spending.

Why it matters to BukkiBooks: BukkiBooks could monitor activity, feature adoption and usage changes to find accounts needing attention early. A rise in failed transactions or a fall in activity could trigger investigation, and behaviour-based groups could receive different communication, education and follow-up.

Sources: Moniepoint (Account Manager and Implementation Officer roles); Paystack (Customer Insights and Customer Directory, Transactions page, Customers page); Bumpa (Customer Analytics, Customer Groups, Business Performance Analytics). All official sources.

5. What the research means

Usage data can act as an early-warning signal. Instead of waiting for customers to complain or stop using the product, Okonkwo can monitor changes in behaviour. A change in usage is a signal to investigate. It is not automatically proof that the customer is at risk. Customer Success still needs to understand why behaviour changed.

6. Key BukkiBooks signals

  • Customer activity: login or activity frequency, active customers, changes in activity
  • Feature usage: use of important features, adoption, prolonged non-use
  • Transaction activity: volume, frequency, changes
  • Usage trends: current against previous usage, significant changes over time
  • Support activity: number of issues, repeated issues, unresolved issues

Signals should not be read in isolation. A customer may simply be low-volume, so what matters is meaningful change or a combination of signals.

7. The Ihuoma example

Signal: an important feature no longer used. Duration: 5 weeks. Investigation: why did usage stop? Root cause: a new store manager had not been trained. Intervention: training, user guide, follow-up and the bulk-upload tool. Outcome: the customer kept using BukkiBooks, then renewed and upgraded.

This is why the system should not label a customer “bad” or “at risk”. The signal should trigger an investigation.

8. RISE framework

R: Research

Usage, feature adoption, transaction activity and behaviour changes can give early signals that a customer needs attention.

I: Insight

Behaviour changes can appear before a customer reports a problem, so Customer Success can intervene earlier if changes are monitored systematically.

S: Strategy

Move from manual checks to automated customer-health monitoring that detects meaningful change, prioritises accounts by risk, directs Customer Success to investigate, and supports targeted intervention.

E: Execution

Monitor, detect, flag, prioritise, investigate, intervene, measure, record.

My research showed that behavioural changes can provide early warning signals. This made me realise that Okonkwo should not wait for customers to report problems. My strategy is an automated monitoring and prioritisation process that lets Customer Success spot meaningful changes earlier, investigate the reason and take targeted action.

9. Five Ps framework

Pattern

Usage and behavioural changes give early signals that attention may be needed.

Pain

Customers may stop getting value from important features, Okonkwo may not detect it early enough, and manual monitoring gets harder as the customer base grows.

Perception

A customer can look active overall while struggling with an important part of the product. The shift is from “Is the customer using BukkiBooks?” to “Are they getting value from it?”

Possibility

Use automation to show where human attention is needed. The goal is not to automate Customer Success, but to let technology find where attention is needed while people understand why and decide what to do.

So what?

If meaningful changes are spotted early, Customer Success can step in before the customer is seriously disengaged or loses value.

10. The deeper finding

Pattern

A customer can stay active overall while important feature usage changes. Ihuoma kept using BukkiBooks but stopped Inventory Reconciliation for 5 weeks.

Pain

Important problems hide inside normal-looking activity, and manual monitoring gets harder as customers increase.

Insight

No complaint does not mean a healthy customer.

Coincidence

The five-week non-use looked like the problem, but it was a symptom.

Root problem: customer level

The new store manager had not been trained, creating an adoption gap.

Root problem: Okonkwo level

No scalable system detected meaningful changes and directed Customer Success to investigate early.

Opportunity: turn customer-usage data into an early-warning system for Customer Success.

11. Proposed solution

Objective: a scalable process that automatically identifies meaningful changes in BukkiBooks usage, prioritises accounts that need attention, and guides Customer Success to the right intervention. Principle: let the system find the customers who need attention so the team can focus on understanding and helping them.

  1. Monitor activity or login, feature usage, transaction or usage volume, support activity and usage trends.
  2. Detect meaningful changes: an important feature unused for a sustained period, a significant drop in activity, low feature adoption, several warning signals together, or repeated support issues. Thresholds are proposed starting points, not research facts, and Okonkwo should validate them with its own historical data.
  3. Prioritise accounts: high (serious or multiple warning signals), medium (concerning changes needing investigation), low (healthy usage). Ihuoma would likely have been high priority.
  4. Customer Success investigates. An alert does not automatically mean risk. Possible reasons: lack of understanding, a new employee, lack of training, a technical problem, a product bug, a changed business process, or a feature no longer relevant.
  5. Take the right action: training, product demonstration, onboarding support, guidance, Support or Product escalation, or a customer conversation. For Ihuoma: training, a guide, follow-up and the bulk-upload tool.
  6. Follow up. Do not stop at “We contacted the customer.” Ask “Did the customer’s behaviour improve?”
  7. Record the outcome: customer, warning signal, duration, priority, reason, action, owner, follow-up date and outcome, building a history that improves the process over time.

13. Proposed metrics and KPIs

The research shows which types of signals to monitor. Okonkwo’s own data will show how much usage is normal and what change is meaningful, so these metrics are proposed for Okonkwo to test and validate.

Feature adoption rate

Customers using the feature ÷ customers expected to use it × 100

Usage decline rate

(Previous usage − current usage) ÷ previous usage × 100

At-risk account rate

At-risk accounts ÷ total active accounts × 100

Intervention rate

Flagged customers contacted ÷ flagged customers × 100

Usage recovery rate

Customers whose usage improved ÷ customers who received intervention × 100

Time to intervention

How long Customer Success takes to act once an account is flagged. The goal is to reduce unnecessary delay.

Retention / renewal rate

Customers renewed ÷ customers due for renewal × 100

14. The KPI rule and validating thresholds

The numbers and thresholds in this KPI system are not statistics from the research. If Okonkwo treats a 30% usage decline as a warning, that does not mean a 30% decline means the customer will churn. It means Okonkwo can test whether a 30% decline is a useful warning signal using its historical data.

  1. Collect 6 to 12 months of historical BukkiBooks customer data.
  2. Establish what normal customer behaviour looks like.
  3. Compare healthy accounts with accounts that had problems.
  4. Identify patterns that appeared before customers disengaged.
  5. Test the proposed warning thresholds.
  6. Monitor false alerts and missed alerts.
  7. Adjust the thresholds based on what the data shows.

This stops Okonkwo choosing arbitrary numbers.

Activity metrics vs outcome KPIs: an activity metric, such as the number of flagged customers contacted, shows what the team did. An outcome KPI, such as the percentage of flagged customers whose usage improved, shows whether it worked. The goal is not to contact more customers, but to help customers recover value and stay successful.

15. Risk and limitation

Usage data alone can mislead. A customer may use the product less often by nature, have a temporary seasonal drop, have changed business processes, have stopped using a feature that is no longer relevant, or have a technical problem. Low usage does not automatically mean an unhealthy customer.

How Okonkwo can address it: combine usage data with support activity, customer feedback, customer conversations, business context and historical usage patterns, and have Customer Success investigate the reason behind an alert before deciding what to do.

16. Customer Success vs Support

Customer Success

Proactive. Focused on adoption, value, health, outcomes, training, education, feature adoption and proactive follow-up. In the case, Chiamaka noticed the unused feature, investigated why and provided training and guidance.

Support

Focused on resolving specific issues: technical problems, product issues, troubleshooting, immediate resolution and escalation. In the case, Support Agent Ifeoma Chukwu resolved a separate invoicing bug within a day.

Working together: Support notices repeated issues, shares them with Customer Success, who judge whether they affect adoption or customer health, and the Product or Technical team is involved when a recurring product problem exists. This gives a stronger picture of the customer’s experience.

17. Claim and proof

Claim: usage data can help identify customers who may need attention. Proof: Moniepoint’s customer-facing roles reference login frequency, transaction volume, support ticket trends, feature adoption and engagement signals when identifying at-risk or low-adoption customers. Paystack gives merchants customer and transaction data, including successful, failed, reversed and abandoned transactions. Bumpa provides analytics on customers, purchasing behaviour, spending and business performance.

What this supports: behaviour and usage data can be used as signals. It does not prove that every usage change means a customer is at risk, which is why Customer Success investigation stays important.

18. Conclusion and recommendation

Customer usage and behavioural data can help fintech and SaaS companies spot changes in engagement, feature adoption, transaction activity and usage patterns that may need attention. Ihuoma Fashion House was still using BukkiBooks, but an important feature had gone unused for five weeks. The problem was not that the customer left, but that part of the product was no longer being adopted. Labelling customers simply “active” or “inactive” is not enough, and manual monitoring becomes hard as the customer base grows.

I recommend that Okonkwo build a data-informed Customer Success early-warning system for BukkiBooks. It should:

  • Monitor customer activity, feature usage, transaction or usage volume, support activity and changes over time.
  • Detect meaningful changes in behaviour and flag accounts that may need investigation.
  • Prioritise accounts by the level of attention required.
  • Let Customer Success investigate the reason before acting.
  • Direct customers to the right intervention: training, guidance, Support or the Product and Technical teams.
  • Track whether the intervention improves usage and adoption.
  • Record outcomes so future Customer Success decisions improve.

The key recommendation is not to automate Customer Success itself, but to use data and automation to show where human attention is needed most.

Okonkwo should move from manually watching customer activity to a scalable, data-informed Customer Success early-warning system. Automation finds where attention is needed. Customer Success understands why and decides what to do.

This would help Okonkwo identify customers like Ihuoma earlier, respond before disengagement becomes a bigger problem, and manage customer health more effectively as BukkiBooks grows.
